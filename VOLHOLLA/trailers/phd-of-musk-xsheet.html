Conform

Put the song on an integer frame grid before a single drawing is made

At 103.10 BPM a beat is 13.967 frames at 24 fps — not an integer, so a bar-locked board drifts. By the last bar the error reaches 14.5 frames, over half a second. Slow the master by 0.235% and every beat becomes exactly 14 frames. The stretch is far below the threshold of audibility and it makes every downbeat land on a whole frame for the entire song.

The gap

48 bars — 1 minute 52 — with nothing written for them

Blocks 1–60 cover bars 1–60. The song runs to bar 107. Bar 61 begins at 2:22.8; the track ends at 4:11.3. The current OUTRO (blocks 58–60, “You brought concepts / I brought hunger / Guess which one found dinner”) lands inside the breakdown — the emptiest 18 seconds in the song, hats gone, onset near zero. As a place to drop the punchline that is superb. As a place to iris out it is 45% too early.

The back half is not filler. Section G — bars 76–107, 74 seconds — is timbrally unlike every earlier section in the song (cosine similarity to the verse bed is −0.82). It is darker, the hats are gone, and a centred lead runs all the way through it. Whatever it is, it is a whole third movement the treatment never saw.

RECOMMENDED

Extend, don't redistribute

Keep block N = bar N exactly as written and author 47 new bars. The treatment already carries the grammar for it: blocks 44–51 are explicit REPRISE variants of 20–27, so the reprise logic is established. D/E/F (bars 58–75) become the climax the breakdown is obviously built for, and G becomes a wordless 32-bar coda — the one stretch where the film can stop making jokes and just follow the nose.

Or cut the song

Edit the master to end at bar 60 for a 2:25 video. Bars 58–65 are already a breakdown, so an ending built there needs only a tail, not a fabricated fade. This is the cheap option and it is not a bad one — but it throws away the strongest structural gift the track offers, which is that the breakdown arrives before the ending rather than at it.

Do not spread 60 blocks evenly across 108 bars. That puts 1.8 bars under each gag, destroys the 1:1 lock, and moves all six section boundaries off the music. The alignment is the asset; stretching it is the one move that spends it for nothing.

Reasoning protocol

The order a model should work in, and what to check at each step

  1. Take the grid as given, not as a variable. Bar n starts at frame 56n in conformed time. Never re-derive tempo, never re-time a block that already has a bar. If a proposed gag does not fit 56 frames, cut the gag, not the bar.
  2. Read the bar's measured state before writing its action. Each row carries LOW, HIGH, LEAD, KEYS and PLANES. A bar with LOW < 0.45 has no bass under it — that is where weight gags fail and float gags land. A bar with HIGH < 0.10 has no hats — fast cutting there reads as noise; hold instead.
  3. Respect the section's identity, not just its energy. A2 and A4 are the same bed (0.98). B1 and B2 are the same bed (0.99). Anything staged on B1 has a guaranteed rhyme available at B2 sixteen bars later, and the treatment already uses it. G rhymes with nothing, so it must not reprise — it is the one section that has to introduce.
  4. Author the 47 missing bars against the same schema. Every new block needs the fields the existing 60 have: a CINEOSIS operation, a physical action, keys, and a multiplane assignment. Then check it against the budget in its row rather than against taste.
  5. Keep the running gags on a schedule, not a whim. The bent blade of grass, the sardine tin, the fishbone grin and the nose-as-compass are the film's only continuity. Place each recurrence on a named bar and verify the spacing is legible — a callback closer than 8 bars reads as a continuity error, further than 32 reads as a new object.
  6. Validate the whole board before rendering anything. Every bar 0–107 has exactly one block; no block spans a section boundary; total frames equals 6048; every gag's key count is at or under its row's budget. Any failure is a board error, not a rendering problem.

Method
Tempo from spectral-flux onset autocorrelation; downbeat by phase search over the flux envelope. Section boundaries from a checkerboard-kernel novelty curve on a bar-synchronous log-mel self-similarity matrix, confirmed against low- and high-band gating. LEAD is the ratio of mid-channel to mid-plus-side energy in the 250–3800 Hz band — it detects a centred lead element, not specifically a voice. Section C's drop to 0.735 means the lead leaves the centre there; whether it stops or merely widens is the one thing on this sheet that wants a human ear.
